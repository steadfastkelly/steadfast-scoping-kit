For account managers

Build a scope from parts, not from scratch.

Everything a proposal needs is here at 2026 rates: 80 services at three levels, seven pre-built packages, hours for production, and the terms. Pick a package, adjust levels, add the companions, paste. Thirty minutes, not three days.

1
Hold the intro call.

Thirty minutes, free, with Taylor or the account lead. One outcome: decide whether The Brief is the right first step. If the client already knows exactly what they want and has done it with us before, skip to step 3.

2
Sell The Brief.

$5,000, two weeks, credited to Stage 1. The Brief page has the client-facing explanation and the infographic. Send the paid kickoff link after the call, never before it.

3
Start from a package.

Seven bundles cover most of what we sell. Load one into the Scope Builder, then remove what the client does not need. Starting from zero is where the hours go.

4
Set the level on each line.

Level 1 refreshes or works from what exists. Level 2 is new work on an approved direction. Level 3 adds strategy, research, custom interaction or regulated rounds. The level test for every service type is on the Levels page. When in doubt between two levels, pick the higher one and say what the lower one would drop.

5
Add the companions.

Every site build carries Site Foundation once, QA, Accessibility & Launch once, and Account Management, Website Build. Every project over $10,000 carries Account Management at the matching tier. Pass-through costs are one line, at cost, with the item named. The builder warns you when one is missing.

6
Write the proposal in this order.

What we heard (four or five sentences in the client's words). One-line thesis. Four strategic imperatives with a "done when" line under each. Stages named as verbs, each with its items and price. Terms. Signature. The price comes after the argument, never before it. The builder exports the item lines and the stage totals; the narrative is yours.

Rule of truth

The catalog price is the price.

Discount only on a named row with a written reason. Partner-agency deals and older client rate cards are priced from the catalog first, then reduced where the contract says so.

Naming

Service Title | L2

Titles in Title Case, levels explained in the description in sentence case. No dashes. Copy descriptions from the catalog as they are; they were written to go in front of a client.

Hours

Hours are the budget.

Every line funds the hours shown at the 2026 card. Hand production the Hours by role total from the builder. Past those hours the line loses money, so flag it before it happens, not after.
